Chandela & Chauhan Dynasties (9th–12th century CE)

Regions: Central & North India | Capitals: Khajuraho and Ajmer

Introduction

The Chandela and Chauhan dynasties were two powerful Rajput lineages that flourished during the medieval period in northern and central India. Both contributed richly to art, culture, and defense against foreign invasions.

Chandela Dynasty

The Chandela rulers controlled Bundelkhand (modern Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh) with their capital at Khajuraho. They are best known for their patronage of temple architecture.

Major Chandela Rulers

  • Yashovarman (c. 925–950 CE)
  • Dhangadeva (c. 950–1002 CE)
  • Vidyadhara (c. 1003–1035 CE)

Chandela Achievements

  • Constructed the world-famous Khajuraho Temples, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
  • Promoted art, sculpture, and literature reflecting Hindu and Jain traditions.
  • Successfully resisted Mahmud of Ghazni’s invasion in the early 11th century.

Chauhan Dynasty

The Chauhans ruled large parts of Rajasthan and Delhi, with Ajmer and later Delhi as their capitals. They were renowned for their valor and conflicts with the early Muslim invaders.

Major Chauhan Rulers

  • Vigraharaja IV (12th century CE)
  • Prithviraj Chauhan (c. 1178–1192 CE)

Chauhan Achievements

  • Prithviraj Chauhan defeated Muhammad Ghori in the First Battle of Tarain (1191 CE).
  • Developed Delhi and Ajmer as major political centers.
  • Promoted Sanskrit and Prakrit literature.

Decline

The Chandela power declined after repeated invasions by the Delhi Sultanate. The Chauhans lost Delhi after the Second Battle of Tarain (1192 CE), marking the beginning of Muslim rule in northern India.
