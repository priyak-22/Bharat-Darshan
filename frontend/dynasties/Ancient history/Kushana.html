Kushana Empire (c. 30 – 375 CE)

Region: Northern India, Afghanistan, and Central Asia | Capital: Purushapura (Peshawar)

Capital

Purushapura (Peshawar), later Mathura

Notable Rulers

  • Kujula Kadphises (Founder)
  • Vima Kadphises
  • Kanishka I (Greatest ruler)
  • Huvishka
  • Vasudeva I

Religion

Religious tolerance prevailed; Buddhism, Hinduism, and Zoroastrianism flourished. Kanishka was a major patron of Mahayana Buddhism.

Administration

The Kushanas established a vast empire connecting India with Central Asia. They adopted a mixed system of Central Asian and Indian administrative models with local governors and tributary rulers.

Achievements

  • Expanded trade through the Silk Route linking India to Rome and China
  • Promoted art and sculpture, leading to the Gandhara and Mathura schools
  • Issued gold coins with Greco-Roman and Indian deities
  • Hosted the Fourth Buddhist Council under Kanishka
  • Encouraged Sanskrit and Greek inscriptions
