Kakatiya & Hoysala Dynasties (11th–14th century CE)

Regions: Deccan Plateau & Southern India | Capitals: Warangal and Halebidu

Introduction

The Kakatiya and Hoysala dynasties emerged as powerful regional kingdoms in South India during the medieval period. Both are remembered for their remarkable contributions to architecture, administration, and cultural development.

Kakatiya Dynasty

The Kakatiyas ruled from Warangal (in present-day Telangana). They unified the Telugu-speaking regions and promoted trade, irrigation, and temple architecture.

Major Kakatiya Rulers

  • Prola II (c. 1110–1158 CE)
  • Rudradeva (c. 1158–1195 CE)
  • Ganapati Deva (c. 1199–1262 CE)
  • Rudrama Devi (c. 1262–1289 CE)
  • Prataparudra II (c. 1289–1323 CE)

Kakatiya Achievements

  • Constructed the famous Warangal Fort and Thousand Pillar Temple at Hanamkonda.
  • Queen Rudrama Devi was one of the few prominent female rulers in Indian history.
  • Encouraged agriculture through extensive irrigation projects like tanks and reservoirs.
  • Promoted Telugu language and literature.

Hoysala Dynasty

The Hoysalas ruled parts of modern-day Karnataka with their capitals at Belur and Halebidu. They are renowned for their exquisite temple architecture and cultural achievements.

Major Hoysala Rulers

  • Vishnuvardhana (c. 1108–1152 CE)
  • Ballala II (c. 1173–1220 CE)
  • Veera Ballala III (c. 1292–1343 CE)

Hoysala Achievements

  • Built magnificent temples such as Chennakesava Temple at Belur and Hoysaleswara Temple at Halebidu.
  • Encouraged Kannada and Sanskrit literature.
  • Developed a distinct architectural style characterized by detailed stone carvings.
  • Maintained political independence until their decline due to Delhi Sultanate invasions.

Decline

The Kakatiyas fell to the forces of the Delhi Sultanate under Muhammad bin Tughlaq in 1323 CE. The Hoysala kingdom weakened after repeated invasions, eventually merging into the Vijayanagara Empire by the mid-14th century.
