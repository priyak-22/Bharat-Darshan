Mewar (Sisodiya Rajput) Kingdom (c. 7th–16th century CE)

Region: South-central Rajasthan | Capital: Chittorgarh, later Udaipur

Introduction

The Mewar Kingdom, ruled by the Sisodiya Rajputs, was one of the most powerful and enduring Rajput states in Rajasthan. Known for its valor, independence, and resistance to foreign domination, Mewar played a central role in preserving Rajput pride and identity.

Major Rulers

  • Bappa Rawal (founder, 8th century CE)
  • Rana Kumbha (1433–1468 CE)
  • Rana Sanga (1508–1528 CE)
  • Maharana Pratap (1572–1597 CE)

Religion

Hinduism (mainly Shaivism and Vaishnavism). The Sisodiyas were patrons of temples and respected Jainism as well.

Administration

The rulers of Mewar followed a monarchical system supported by local chieftains. The kingdom maintained strong fortresses like Chittorgarh and Kumbhalgarh for defense. Land grants were given to nobles in exchange for military service, and village panchayats handled local administration.

Achievements

  • Rana Kumbha built several magnificent forts and temples, including the Kumbhalgarh Fort.
  • Maharana Pratap’s valor in the Battle of Haldighati (1576 CE) became legendary.
  • Promoted Rajput architecture, literature, and painting traditions.
  • Maintained independence and resisted Mughal dominance longer than most Rajput states.
