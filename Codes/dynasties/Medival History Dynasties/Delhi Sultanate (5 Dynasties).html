Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526 CE)

Region: Northern India | Capital: Delhi

Introduction

The Delhi Sultanate was a major Islamic empire that ruled northern India from the early 13th to the early 16th century. It consisted of five successive dynasties — Slave, Khilji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, and Lodi — each contributing to the political and cultural foundation of medieval India.

1. Slave Dynasty (1206–1290 CE)

Founded by Qutb-ud-din Aibak, a former slave of Muhammad Ghori. The dynasty laid the foundation of Muslim rule in India with Delhi as its capital.

  • Important rulers: Qutb-ud-din Aibak, Iltutmish, Razia Sultana, Balban.
  • Achievements: Construction of Qutub Minar and consolidation of power in northern India.

2. Khilji Dynasty (1290–1320 CE)

The Khiljis expanded the empire through military conquests and economic reforms. They extended control over Gujarat, Rajasthan, and southern India.

  • Important rulers: Jalal-ud-din Khilji, Alauddin Khilji.
  • Achievements: Market control reforms, successful campaigns in the Deccan, and defense against Mongol invasions.

3. Tughlaq Dynasty (1320–1414 CE)

The Tughlaqs introduced administrative and agricultural reforms but faced challenges due to overambitious projects.

  • Important rulers: Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq, Muhammad bin Tughlaq, Firoz Shah Tughlaq.
  • Achievements: Development of irrigation systems, establishment of new towns, and reforms in taxation and currency.

4. Sayyid Dynasty (1414–1451 CE)

The Sayyid rulers were relatively weak and ruled as vassals under the Timurids. Their rule marked a transitional phase before the rise of the Lodis.

  • Important rulers: Khizr Khan, Mubarak Shah.
  • Achievements: Maintenance of order in Delhi and revival of local administration.

5. Lodi Dynasty (1451–1526 CE)

The Lodi dynasty was the last of the Delhi Sultanate and the first Afghan dynasty to rule Delhi. It ended with the arrival of Babur and the establishment of the Mughal Empire.

  • Important rulers: Bahlol Lodi, Sikandar Lodi, Ibrahim Lodi.
  • Achievements: Urban development, trade promotion, and expansion of agriculture.

Decline

The Delhi Sultanate declined due to internal conflicts, weak successors, and invasions. The defeat of Ibrahim Lodi by Babur in the First Battle of Panipat (1526 CE) marked the end of the Sultanate and the beginning of Mughal rule.
