The Saka Dynasty

Overview

The Sakas, also known as the Scythians, were Central Asian nomadic tribes who migrated into India around the 2nd century BCE. They established several kingdoms across western and northwestern India after the decline of the Indo-Greeks.

Historical Highlights

  • Period: c. 2nd century BCE – 4th century CE
  • Region: Western and Northwestern India (especially Gujarat, Rajasthan, and Malwa)
  • Capitals: Ujjain, Taxila
  • Notable Rulers: Maues (Moga), Azes I, Azes II, Nahapana, Chashtana, Rudradaman I
  • Contemporaries: Indo-Greeks, Satavahanas, Kushanas

Culture and Achievements

The Sakas were great patrons of trade and art. Under Rudradaman I, Sanskrit inscriptions gained prominence, such as the famous Junagadh Rock Inscription. They helped maintain trade routes connecting India with Central Asia and Rome.

Decline

The Saka power gradually declined after repeated defeats by the Satavahanas and later the Guptas. By the 4th century CE, most Saka territories were absorbed into the Gupta Empire.

Map and Influence

The Saka territories covered parts of modern-day Gujarat, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Punjab.
