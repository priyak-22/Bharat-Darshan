India’s Freedom Movement

Trace India’s struggle for independence — from early revolts to the birth of a free nation in 1947.

Revolt of 1857

Year: 1857

Leaders: Mangal Pandey, Rani Lakshmibai, Bahadur Shah Zafar

Significance: India’s first major rebellion against British rule; marked the beginning of national awakening.

Formation of Indian National Congress

Year: 1885

Leaders: A.O. Hume, W.C. Bonnerjee, Dadabhai Naoroji

Significance: Provided a platform for Indians to voice political aspirations and demand reforms.

Non-Cooperation Movement

Year: 1920–22

Leader: Mahatma Gandhi

Significance: Encouraged Indians to boycott British institutions and inspired nationwide unity.

Civil Disobedience Movement

Year: 1930–34

Event: Salt March (Dandi March)

Significance: Marked a mass protest against unjust British salt laws, symbolizing self-reliance and resistance.

Quit India Movement

Year: 1942

Leader: Mahatma Gandhi

Significance: A final call for “Do or Die” against British rule, uniting Indians across the nation.

Independence of India

Date: August 15, 1947

Significance: India achieved freedom after centuries of struggle, marking the birth of the world’s largest democracy.
