Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919 CE)

Year: 1919 CE | Region: Amritsar, Punjab

Background

The massacre took place against the backdrop of the repressive Rowlatt Act (1919), which allowed the British government to detain individuals without trial. This law sparked widespread protests across India, particularly in Punjab.

The Incident

On April 13, 1919, thousands of men, women, and children gathered peacefully at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar to protest the Rowlatt Act. British troops under General Reginald Dyer blocked the exits and opened fire without warning.

According to official figures, around 379 people were killed and over 1,200 wounded, though Indian estimates put the number much higher.

Aftermath

  • Widespread outrage and grief swept across India.
  • Rabindranath Tagore renounced his knighthood in protest.
  • The British set up the Hunter Commission to investigate the incident.
  • The event shattered Indian faith in British justice and marked a turning point in the freedom struggle.

Impact and Significance

  • Unified Indians across communities in condemning British rule.
  • Strengthened Mahatma Gandhi’s call for Non-Cooperation and complete independence.
  • Exposed the brutality of colonial rule to the world.
