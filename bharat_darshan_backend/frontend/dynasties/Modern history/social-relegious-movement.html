Social & Religious Reform Movements (19th Century)

Period: 19th Century | Region: Across India

Background

The 19th century in India witnessed a wave of social and religious reform movements aimed at eradicating social evils, modernizing society, and reviving Indian traditions in the light of rational thought and education. These movements emerged under the influence of Western education and growing national consciousness.

Major Reformers and Their Contributions

  • Raja Ram Mohan Roy: Founded the Brahmo Samaj (1828) and led campaigns against sati, child marriage, and for women’s education and widow remarriage.
  • Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar: Advocated widow remarriage and promoted women’s education; instrumental in the Widow Remarriage Act of 1856.
  • Swami Dayanand Saraswati: Founded the Arya Samaj (1875) to promote Vedic values, social equality, and education through the motto “Back to the Vedas.”
  • Syed Ahmad Khan: Founded the Aligarh Movement and the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College (later Aligarh Muslim University) to modernize Muslim education and thought.
  • Swami Vivekananda: Spread the message of universal brotherhood and the spiritual heritage of India through the Ramakrishna Mission (1897).

Objectives

  • Eliminate social evils like sati, untouchability, and child marriage.
  • Promote women’s rights, education, and equality.
  • Revive rational and ethical aspects of religion while rejecting blind superstition.
  • Encourage modern education and national awakening.

Impact and Significance

  • Laid the foundation for modern Indian society based on equality and reform.
  • Promoted inter-religious understanding and tolerance.
  • Inspired socio-political awakening and paved the way for India’s national movement.
  • Strengthened education and intellectual revival across the country.
