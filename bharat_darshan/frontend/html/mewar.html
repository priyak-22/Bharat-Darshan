Mewar Dynasty (Sisodia Rajputs)

Capital

The original capital of Mewar was Chittorgarh, one of the largest fort complexes in India. Due to repeated sieges, Maharana Udai Singh II founded Udaipur in 1559 as the new capital.

View Chittorgarh Fort

Background

Mewar was a powerful Rajput kingdom in present-day Rajasthan ruled by the Sisodia clan. It emerged as one of the most respected and resilient Rajput states in medieval India. The dynasty claimed descent from the ancient Guhila lineage.

Founding and Rise

The dynasty traces its origin to Bappa Rawal in the 8th century. Over time, rulers like Rana Kumbha and Rana Sanga expanded Mewar’s territory and influence. By the 15th century, Mewar became a leading Rajput power.

Religion

The rulers were devoted Hindus and worshipped Eklingji (Shiva) as the supreme deity. The Maharana ruled as the representative of Eklingji. Jainism also flourished, and temples were patronized across the kingdom.

Economy and Administration

Agriculture was the backbone of the economy. Land revenue was the main source of income. Lakes and irrigation systems were developed to support farming. Administration followed a feudal system where local chiefs governed territories under the Maharana.

Notable Rulers

Bappa Rawal

Founder of the dynasty and early consolidator of power.

Rana Kumbha

Built 32 forts including Kumbhalgarh. Encouraged art, architecture, and literature.

Rana Sanga

United Rajputs and fought Babur in the Battle of Khanwa (1527).

Maharana Pratap

Known for resisting Mughal Emperor Akbar. Fought the famous Battle of Haldighati (1576).

Political History

Mewar faced invasions from Alauddin Khalji (1303) and Bahadur Shah of Gujarat (1535). It resisted Mughal domination under Maharana Pratap. Eventually, a treaty was signed during Amar Singh I’s reign.

Achievements

  • Construction of massive hill forts
  • Preservation of Rajput independence
  • Development of lake systems in Udaipur
  • Promotion of miniature painting traditions

Intangible Heritage

Mewar is known for its strong Rajput code of honor, bravery traditions, folk ballads celebrating Maharana Pratap, and the Mewar school of miniature paintings. Traditional festivals and rituals continue to preserve its legacy.

Decline and Fall

Continuous warfare weakened the kingdom economically. During the British period, Mewar became a princely state under indirect rule. After independence in 1947, it merged into Rajasthan.
