Advent of the British (1615 CE)

Period: 1615 CE | Region: Surat, Madras, Bombay, Calcutta

Background

The British East India Company arrived in India in 1615 CE when Sir Thomas Roe, the English ambassador to Mughal Emperor Jahangir, secured permission to trade and establish factories. This marked the beginning of British commercial presence in India, which later evolved into political dominance.

Major Trading Posts

  • Surat: The first British trading post in India, established in 1615 CE under Mughal permission.
  • Madras (Chennai): Became a major center for textile trade; Fort St. George was established here.
  • Bombay (Mumbai): Acquired from the Portuguese in 1661 CE; developed into a key western port.
  • Calcutta (Kolkata): Founded in 1690 CE; became the capital of British India by the 18th century.

Administration

The British East India Company initially functioned as a commercial body, governed by directors in London and company officials in India. Over time, it developed administrative and military powers to protect trade interests, laying the groundwork for British colonial rule.

Trade and Economy

  • Traded cotton, silk, indigo, spices, and saltpetre.
  • Established fortified settlements for protection and control.
  • Developed ports and shipping networks linking India with Europe and Southeast Asia.

Impact and Significance

  • Marked the beginning of long-term British involvement in Indian politics and economy.
  • Laid the foundation for British colonial expansion through alliances and wars.
  • Shifted the balance of trade and introduced new administrative systems.
  • Transformed India into a key part of the global British Empire.
