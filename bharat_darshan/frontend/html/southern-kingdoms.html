Southern Kingdoms

(Chera, Pandya, Pallava, Chalukya, and Chola)

The Southern dynasties were among the most influential in peninsular India. They shaped trade, culture, and temple architecture while maintaining distinct regional identities. Their rule spanned from early historic times to the medieval period.

Chera Dynasty (up to 12th century CE)

Capital: Vanji (Karur) or Mahodayapuram (Makotai)

Region: Present-day Kerala and western Tamil Nadu

Highlights: The Cheras were known for maritime trade with the Romans, and for promoting Tamil literature during the Sangam age.

Pandya Dynasty (up to 14th century CE)

Capital: Madurai (Tamil Nadu)

Region: Southern Tamil Nadu

Highlights: Among the oldest Tamil dynasties, the Pandyas supported literature and temple architecture. Madurai became a major cultural and religious center.

Pallava Dynasty (275–897 CE)

Capital: Kanchipuram (Tamil Nadu)

Region: Northern Tamil Nadu

Highlights: Known for early Dravidian architecture; built monuments at Mahabalipuram; promoted Sanskrit and Tamil culture.

Chalukya Dynasty (6th–12th century CE)

Capital: Badami (Vatapi), later Kalyani (present-day Karnataka)

Region: Deccan region, mainly Karnataka

Highlights: The Chalukyas developed unique temple architecture (e.g., Aihole, Pattadakal) and contributed to art and administration in the Deccan.

Chola Dynasty (9th–13th century CE)

Capitals: Thanjavur (Tanjore) and later Gangaikonda Cholapuram

Region: Tamil Nadu and parts of Sri Lanka

Highlights: The Cholas built a powerful maritime empire, spread Indian culture overseas, and built the Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur.
