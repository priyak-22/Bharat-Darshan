Decline of the Mughal Empire

Period: 1707–1857 CE | Region: Indian Subcontinent

Background

The decline of the Mughal Empire began after the death of Aurangzeb in 1707 CE. Weak successors, internal conflicts, and administrative inefficiency gradually weakened central authority.

Major Causes

  • Weak Rulers: Later Mughal emperors lacked leadership and control.
  • Jagirdari Crisis: Revenue system collapsed due to corruption and over-assignment.
  • Regional Revolts: Rise of Marathas, Sikhs, Jats, and Rajputs.
  • Foreign Invasions: Attacks by Nadir Shah (1739) and Ahmad Shah Abdali.

Administrative Decline

The Mughal administrative system became inefficient due to factionalism at court, military weakness, and lack of financial resources. Provincial governors started acting independently.

Role of European Powers

The weakening Mughal authority allowed European trading companies, especially the British East India Company, to interfere in Indian politics and gain territorial control.

End of the Empire

After the Revolt of 1857, the British formally ended Mughal rule. Emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar was exiled, marking the final collapse of the Mughal Empire.

Impact and Significance

  • Led to the rise of regional powers across India.
  • Created conditions for British colonial dominance.
  • Ended centralized imperial rule in India.
  • Marked a major political transition in Indian history.
