Indo-Greek Kingdoms (c. 180 BCE – 10 CE)

Region: Northwestern India and Afghanistan | Capitals: Taxila, Sagala (Sialkot)

Capitals

Taxila and Sagala (modern Sialkot)

Notable Rulers

Demetrius I, Menander (Milinda), Antialcidas, Apollodotus I

Religion

Hellenism, Buddhism, and local Indian traditions blended; Menander adopted Buddhism.

Administration

The Indo-Greeks ruled through a mix of Greek and Indian systems, using bilingual inscriptions and coins. They had urban centers, fortified cities, and maintained a strong trade-based economy with Hellenistic influence in art and governance.

Achievements

  • Introduced Greek artistic and architectural styles to India.
  • Issued bilingual coins with Greek and Prakrit legends.
  • Encouraged the fusion of Greek and Indian cultures (Greco-Buddhist art).
  • Supported Buddhism — King Menander was later revered in Buddhist texts as “Milinda.”
