Advent of the Dutch (1605 CE)

Period: 1605 CE | Region: Coastal India (Masulipatnam, Pulicat, Surat)

Background

The Dutch East India Company (VOC) arrived in India in 1605 CE and established their first trading post at Masulipatnam on the eastern coast. Their aim was to dominate the spice and textile trade and challenge the Portuguese monopoly in Asia.

Major Trading Posts

  • Masulipatnam: The first Dutch factory in India, established in 1605 CE.
  • Pulicat: Became the main Dutch headquarters on the Coromandel Coast.
  • Surat: Important western port where the Dutch traded textiles and spices.

Administration

The Dutch East India Company (VOC) governed its Indian operations through appointed Governors and Directors. Their control was primarily commercial, focused on securing profitable trade agreements and establishing coastal forts to protect their factories.

Trade and Economy

  • Traded spices, indigo, saltpetre, and textiles from Indian ports.
  • Engaged in trade with Southeast Asian colonies through the Indian Ocean network.
  • Maintained a strong naval presence to protect trading interests.

Decline and Impact

  • Faced growing competition from the English and French companies.
  • Defeated by the English in naval battles by the late 17th century.
  • Eventually withdrew from India after losing dominance in trade and territory.
  • Contributed to the spread of European trade practices and naval technology.
