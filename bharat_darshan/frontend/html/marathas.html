Maratha Empire (1674–1818 CE)

Region: Western India → Deccan Plateau → Central & Northern India

Foundation of the Empire

The Maratha Empire was founded by Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj in 1674 after his coronation at Raigad Fort . He established an independent kingdom challenging Mughal dominance in the Deccan region.

Early Life of Shivaji

Shivaji was born in 1630 at Shivneri Fort . Inspired by his mother Jijabai and mentor Dadoji Konddev, he developed strong administrative and military skills.

Military Strategy

The Marathas became famous for their guerrilla warfare strategy known as Ganimi Kava. They used hill forts such as Pratapgad Fort and Sinhagad Fort as strategic strongholds.

  • Rapid cavalry movements
  • Strong fort defense system
  • Naval development under Kanhoji Angre
  • Surprise attacks against larger armies

Administration System

Shivaji introduced the Ashta Pradhan Mandal (Council of Eight Ministers). It included:

  • Peshwa (Prime Minister)
  • Amatya (Finance Minister)
  • Senapati (Military Commander)
  • Mantri (Interior Minister)
  • Sumant (Foreign Affairs)
  • Sachiv (Secretary)
  • Nyayadhish (Chief Justice)
  • Panditrao (Religious Affairs)

Expansion Under the Peshwas

After Shivaji’s death, real power gradually shifted to the Peshwas. Pune became the administrative center of the empire. The Marathas expanded into Malwa, Gujarat, Bundelkhand, and Delhi.

By the mid-18th century, the Marathas were the dominant power in India.

Major Battles

  • Battle of Pratapgad (1659)
  • Siege of Purandar (1665)
  • Third Battle of Panipat (1761)
  • Anglo-Maratha Wars (1775–1818)

Third Battle of Panipat (1761)

The Marathas faced Ahmad Shah Abdali in northern India. Though initially powerful, the Maratha forces suffered a major defeat. This battle weakened their political dominance but did not end the empire immediately.

Economic System

Revenue collection included:

  • Chauth – One-fourth tax from territories
  • Sardeshmukhi – Additional 10% levy

Agriculture remained the backbone of the economy, while western coastal trade expanded significantly.

Decline and End

Internal conflicts among Maratha chiefs and three Anglo-Maratha Wars weakened the empire. In 1818, after the Third Anglo-Maratha War, the British East India Company took control of Maratha territories.

Cultural Legacy

The Maratha period strengthened regional identity in Maharashtra. Fort architecture, temple restoration, and Marathi literature flourished. Today, sites like Raigad Fort stand as symbols of Maratha pride.
