Chola Dynasty (c. 850–1279 CE)

Region: Tamil Nadu and parts of South India

Capital

The early Chola capital was Thanjavur. Later, Gangaikonda Cholapuram became the imperial capital under Rajendra I. These cities were major centers of administration, culture, and temple architecture.

Background

The Cholas were one of the longest-ruling dynasties in South India. Although mentioned in Sangam literature, the imperial phase of the dynasty began in the 9th century under Vijayalaya Chola. They established a powerful and organized state in southern India.

Founding and Rise

Vijayalaya Chola captured Thanjavur around 850 CE. Under rulers like Rajaraja I and Rajendra I, the empire expanded across South India, Sri Lanka, and parts of Southeast Asia. The Cholas built a strong navy and became a dominant maritime power.

Religion

The Cholas were devoted followers of Shaivism. They constructed grand temples dedicated to Lord Shiva. Vaishnavism and other religious traditions also coexisted during their rule.

Economy and Administration

The Chola economy was agrarian but supported by trade and maritime commerce. They maintained strong trade connections with Southeast Asia and China. The administration was well-organized, with local self-government through village assemblies (Sabhas and Urs).

Notable Rulers

  • Vijayalaya Chola – Founder of the Imperial Chola line.
  • Rajaraja I – Expanded the empire and built Brihadeeswara Temple.
  • Rajendra I – Led naval expeditions to Southeast Asia.
  • Kulothunga I – Strengthened administration and trade networks.

Political History and Achievements

The Cholas defeated the Pandyas and Cheras and established supremacy in South India. Rajendra I’s naval campaign reached Srivijaya in Southeast Asia. Their reign marked a golden age of South Indian art, architecture, and governance.

Cultural Heritage

Tangible Heritage

Brihadeeswara Temple

Built by Rajaraja I, this UNESCO-listed temple is one of the greatest examples of Dravidian architecture.

Gangaikonda Cholapuram

Constructed by Rajendra I to commemorate his northern victories. It reflects advanced temple engineering and artistic excellence.

Chola Bronze Art

The Chola period is renowned for its exquisite bronze sculptures, especially the iconic Nataraja (Shiva as cosmic dancer).

Intangible Heritage

The Cholas promoted Tamil literature, temple rituals, and classical arts such as Bharatanatyam. Their administrative system of local self-government is considered highly advanced for its time.

Decline and Fall

The dynasty declined in the 13th century due to internal conflicts and the resurgence of the Pandyas. By 1279 CE, the Chola Empire came to an end.

Historical Sources and Evidence

Information about the Cholas comes from temple inscriptions, copper plate grants, Tamil literature, and foreign accounts from Chinese and Arab traders.
