Revolt of 1857

Year: 1857 CE | Region: Northern and Central India

Background

The Revolt of 1857 was the first large-scale armed uprising against British rule in India. It began as a mutiny of sepoys in the British East India Company’s army but soon grew into a widespread rebellion involving rulers, peasants, and citizens dissatisfied with colonial policies.

Main Causes

  • Political: Annexation policies like the Doctrine of Lapse and dethronement of Indian rulers.
  • Economic: Heavy taxation, decline of traditional industries, and land confiscations.
  • Social & Religious: Fear of forced conversion and interference in traditional customs.
  • Military: Discrimination against Indian soldiers and introduction of the Enfield rifle cartridges rumored to be greased with cow and pig fat.

Major Centers and Leaders

  • Delhi: Bahadur Shah II (symbolic leader)
  • Kanpur: Nana Sahib
  • Lucknow: Begum Hazrat Mahal
  • Jhansi: Rani Lakshmibai
  • Bareilly: Khan Bahadur Khan
  • Bihar: Kunwar Singh

Course of the Revolt

The revolt began on May 10, 1857, in Meerut, when Indian sepoys refused to use the new cartridges and mutinied against their British officers. The rebels marched to Delhi, declaring Bahadur Shah II as their emperor. The rebellion soon spread across northern and central India. However, due to lack of coordination, limited resources, and superior British military strength, the uprising was suppressed by mid-1858.

Consequences

  • The British East India Company was abolished; governance transferred to the British Crown (Government of India Act, 1858).
  • Queen Victoria’s Proclamation promised religious tolerance and non-interference in Indian customs.
  • Reorganization of the army and strengthening of British control.
  • Marked the beginning of Indian nationalism and a new phase of resistance.
