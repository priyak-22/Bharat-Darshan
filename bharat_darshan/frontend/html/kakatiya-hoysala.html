Kakatiya and Hoysala Dynasties (c. 11th–14th Century CE)

Regions: Telangana–Andhra region and Karnataka region (South India)

Kakatiya Dynasty

The Kakatiya dynasty ruled large parts of present-day Telangana and Andhra Pradesh between the 12th and 14th centuries CE. Initially feudatories of the Western Chalukyas, they later established an independent kingdom with Warangal as their capital. The dynasty is known for its administrative reforms, irrigation projects, and temple architecture.

Capital

The capital was Orugallu (modern Warangal), protected by massive fortifications and concentric defensive walls.

Founding and Rise

The dynasty emerged under Prola II and gained independence under Rudradeva. The kingdom reached its height under Ganapati Deva and Rani Rudramadevi. Prataparudra II was the last major ruler before the Delhi Sultanate invasion.

Religion

The Kakatiyas were primarily Shaivites but supported Vaishnavism and Jainism. Temple construction and patronage of Sanskrit and Telugu literature flourished.

Economy and Administration

Agriculture was strengthened through large irrigation tanks such as the Ramappa Lake. The Nayankara system granted land to military chiefs in exchange for service. Trade in diamonds, textiles, and horses contributed to revenue.

Notable Rulers

  • Rudradeva – Consolidated independence.
  • Ganapati Deva – Expanded the kingdom significantly.
  • Rani Rudramadevi – One of the few prominent female rulers in medieval India.
  • Prataparudra II – Faced invasion by Alauddin Khalji’s forces.

Cultural Heritage

Tangible Heritage

Ramappa Temple

Built in 1213 CE, this UNESCO-recognized temple is known for its floating bricks and intricate carvings.

Warangal Fort

A massive fortified structure with impressive gateways known as Kakatiya Kala Thoranam.

Intangible Heritage

The Kakatiya period encouraged Telugu literature, temple dance traditions, and administrative innovations such as the Nayankara system.

Decline

In 1323 CE, the kingdom was conquered by the Delhi Sultanate under Muhammad bin Tughlaq, leading to the fall of the dynasty.

Hoysala Dynasty

The Hoysala dynasty ruled much of Karnataka between the 11th and 14th centuries CE. They are renowned for their unique temple architecture built using soapstone, characterized by intricate carvings and star-shaped platforms.

Capital

The early capital was Belur, later shifted to Halebidu (Dwarasamudra).

Founding and Rise

The dynasty was founded by Nripa Kama II. It gained prominence under Vishnuvardhana, who expanded territory and patronized temple building.

Religion

Initially Jain, the Hoysalas later became strong patrons of Vaishnavism and Shaivism. They supported scholars like Ramanujacharya.

Economy and Administration

Agriculture and trade were major sources of income. The administration was well organized with provincial governors and local assemblies.

Notable Rulers

  • Vishnuvardhana – Builder of Chennakesava Temple.
  • Ballala II – Expanded Hoysala power.
  • Veera Ballala III – Last significant ruler.

Cultural Heritage

Tangible Heritage

Chennakesava Temple

Built in 1117 CE, it represents the finest example of Hoysala architecture.

Hoysaleswara Temple

Known for detailed sculptures depicting scenes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata.

Intangible Heritage

The Hoysalas promoted Kannada literature, temple rituals, and classical art traditions in southern India.

Decline

Continuous conflicts with the Delhi Sultanate and the rise of the Vijayanagara Empire led to the gradual decline of Hoysala power in the 14th century.

Historical Sources and Evidence

Information about both dynasties comes from temple inscriptions, copper plates, literary works, and archaeological remains of forts and temples.
