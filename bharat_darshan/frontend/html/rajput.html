Rajput Kingdoms (c. 7th–16th century CE)

Region: Northern and Western India — mainly Rajasthan, Gujarat, and parts of Madhya Pradesh

Major Kingdoms

Chauhans of Ajmer, Rathores of Marwar, Sisodias of Mewar, Paramaras of Malwa, and Chandels of Bundelkhand

Notable Rulers

Prithviraj Chauhan, Rana Kumbha, Rana Sanga, Raja Bhoja, and Hammir Dev Chauhan

Religion

Hinduism (mainly devoted to Shaivism and Vaishnavism), with respect for Jainism

Administration

The Rajput kingdoms followed a feudal system. Land was divided among nobles who provided military service to their kings. They maintained strong forts, trained armies, and followed codes of honor (*Rajput Dharma*). Each kingdom had its own coinage and local administration.

Achievements

  • Built grand forts and palaces such as Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, and Mehrangarh.
  • Developed a rich culture of art, music, and heroic literature.
  • Played a major role in resisting invasions during the early Islamic conquests.
  • Encouraged temple architecture and sculpture across Rajasthan and Central India.
