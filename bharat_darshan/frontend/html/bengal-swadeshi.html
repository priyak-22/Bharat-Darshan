Partition of Bengal & Swadeshi Movement (1905–1911 CE)

Period: 1905–1911 CE | Region: Bengal and Across India

Background

In 1905, the British Viceroy Lord Curzon announced the partition of Bengal, citing administrative efficiency. However, it was widely seen as an attempt to divide Hindus and Muslims and weaken the growing nationalist movement.

Reaction and Protests

The partition triggered massive protests across Bengal and India. Leaders like Surendranath Banerjee, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, and Bipin Chandra Pal mobilized people against the decision through meetings, boycotts, and demonstrations.

The Swadeshi Movement

  • Promoted the use of Indian-made goods and the boycott of British products.
  • Encouraged national education, handloom industries, and self-reliance.
  • Spread nationalist songs, literature, and symbols across the country.

Key Leaders

  • Bal Gangadhar Tilak
  • Bipin Chandra Pal
  • Lala Lajpat Rai
  • Aurobindo Ghosh
  • Surendranath Banerjee

Outcome and Significance

  • The partition was annulled in 1911 CE due to widespread protests.
  • Marked a shift from moderate to assertive nationalism in India.
  • Gave rise to indigenous industries and the idea of economic self-reliance.
  • Inspired future national movements across India.
