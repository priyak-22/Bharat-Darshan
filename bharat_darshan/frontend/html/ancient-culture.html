{% load static %} Ancient & Early Medieval Cultural Heritage of India

Nanda Dynasty

c. 345 – 321 BCE

Tangible Heritage

  • Fortified city of Pataliputra
  • Punch-marked silver coins
  • Royal treasuries

Intangible Heritage

  • Highly centralized administration
  • Strong taxation system
  • Large standing army

Maurya Empire

321 – 185 BCE

Tangible Heritage

  • Ashokan pillars and edicts
  • Sanchi and Bharhut stupas
  • Palace architecture

Intangible Heritage

  • Dhamma ideology
  • Spread of Buddhism
  • Welfare-based governance

Shunga Dynasty

185 – 73 BCE

Tangible Heritage

  • Bharhut stupa railings
  • Early stone sculptures

Intangible Heritage

  • Brahmanical revival
  • Vedic ritual traditions

Kanva Dynasty

73 – 28 BCE

Tangible Heritage

  • Continuation of Shunga art

Intangible Heritage

  • Political continuity
  • Court traditions

Indo-Greek Kingdom

2nd – 1st Century BCE

Tangible Heritage

  • Greek-style coins
  • Gandhara sculptures

Intangible Heritage

  • Hellenistic–Indian cultural fusion
  • Realistic art tradition

Indo-Parthians

1st BCE – 1st CE

Tangible Heritage

  • Coins and inscriptions

Intangible Heritage

  • Cross-cultural governance
  • Trade administration

Satavahana Dynasty

1st BCE – 3rd CE

Tangible Heritage

  • Amaravati stupa
  • Nasik and Karle caves

Intangible Heritage

  • Prakrit language
  • Maritime trade culture

Vakataka Dynasty

3rd – 5th Century CE

Tangible Heritage

  • Ajanta cave paintings

Intangible Heritage

  • Royal patronage of arts
  • Cultural continuity
