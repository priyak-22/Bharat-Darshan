Cultural Heritage of India

Dynasty-wise cultural heritage from 300 BCE to 1947 CE

Nanda Dynasty (c. 345 BCE – 322 BCE)

Tangible Heritage

  • Fortified cities and administrative centers
  • Punch-marked silver coins
  • Early urban architecture of Magadha

Intangible Heritage

  • Centralized governance system
  • Patronage to Jainism and Buddhism
  • Early economic administration
Maurya Dynasty (322 BCE – 185 BCE)

Tangible Heritage

  • Ashokan pillars and rock edicts
  • Sanchi and Bharhut stupas
  • Polished stone sculptures

Intangible Heritage

  • Dhamma and moral governance
  • Spread of Buddhism
  • Arthashastra traditions
Shunga Dynasty (185 BCE – 73 BCE)

Tangible Heritage

  • Expansion of Sanchi Stupa
  • Railings and gateways
  • Terracotta art

Intangible Heritage

  • Revival of Brahmanical traditions
  • Patronage of Sanskrit learning
  • Early classical art themes
Kanva Dynasty (73 BCE – 28 BCE)

Tangible Heritage

  • Continuation of Shunga architecture
  • Coinage traditions

Intangible Heritage

  • Vedic rituals
  • Sanskrit scholarship
Indo-Greek Kingdom (200 BCE – 10 CE)

Tangible Heritage

  • Greek-style coins
  • Gandhara art foundations

Intangible Heritage

  • Hellenistic cultural influence
  • Fusion of Greek and Indian ideas
Shaka (Scythians) (1st Century BCE – 4th Century CE)

Tangible Heritage

  • Western Indian coins
  • Rock-cut caves

Intangible Heritage

  • Administrative systems
  • Trade networks
Satavahana Dynasty (1st Century BCE – 3rd Century CE)

Tangible Heritage

  • Amaravati and Nashik caves
  • Stupa architecture

Intangible Heritage

  • Prakrit literature
  • Trade and maritime culture
Kushan Dynasty (1st – 3rd Century CE)

Tangible Heritage

  • Gandhara and Mathura art
  • Gold coins

Intangible Heritage

  • Mahayana Buddhism
  • Silk Route trade culture
Gupta Dynasty (320 CE – 550 CE)

Tangible Heritage

  • Ajanta caves
  • Temple architecture
  • Gold coinage

Intangible Heritage

  • Classical Sanskrit literature
  • Advances in science and mathematics
  • Hindu philosophical systems
Harsha Empire (606 CE – 647 CE)

Tangible Heritage

  • Monasteries and stupas
  • Urban centers

Intangible Heritage

  • Patronage of learning
  • Buddhist-Hindu harmony
  • Sanskrit drama
