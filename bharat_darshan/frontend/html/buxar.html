Battle of Buxar (1764 CE)

Period: 1764 CE | Region: Buxar (on the banks of River Ganga, Bihar)

Background

The Battle of Buxar was fought on 22 October 1764 between the British East India Company and the combined forces of Mir Qasim (Nawab of Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daula (Nawab of Awadh), and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II. It was a decisive victory for the British and consolidated their control over Bengal and adjoining regions.

Causes

  • Mir Qasim’s attempt to free Bengal from British interference after his conflict with the East India Company.
  • British expansion and trade privileges that disrupted local economies.
  • Formation of an anti-British alliance among Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula, and Shah Alam II.

Main Events

The British army under Major Hector Munro defeated the combined Indian forces near Buxar. Despite numerical superiority, the allied forces suffered from poor coordination, while the British displayed disciplined artillery and superior military strategy.

Notable Figures

  • Major Hector Munro: British commander who led the East India Company’s troops to victory.
  • Mir Qasim: Nawab of Bengal who attempted to resist British control.
  • Shuja-ud-Daula: Nawab of Awadh and key ally in the anti-British coalition.
  • Shah Alam II: Mughal Emperor whose defeat weakened imperial authority further.

Outcome

The British victory at Buxar confirmed their political dominance in eastern India. Shah Alam II was forced to grant the Diwani rights of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa to the British East India Company through the Treaty of Allahabad (1765).

Impact and Significance

  • Marked the beginning of British political rule in India.
  • Made the East India Company a major territorial power.
  • Reduced the Mughal Emperor to a symbolic ruler.
  • Strengthened British control over revenue and administration.
