Khilji Dynasty (1290–1320 CE)

Region: Northern India – Delhi Sultanate

Capital

The capital of the Khilji Dynasty was Delhi. The rulers governed from the Delhi Sultanate’s established administrative center and strengthened its military and political structure.

Background

The Khilji Dynasty was the second ruling dynasty of the Delhi Sultanate. It was founded by Jalal-ud-din Firuz Khilji in 1290 CE after the decline of the Mamluk (Slave) Dynasty. The Khiljis were of Turkic origin but had long been settled in Afghanistan before coming to India.

Founding and Rise

Jalal-ud-din Firuz Khilji established the dynasty by overthrowing the last ruler of the Mamluk dynasty. However, the empire reached its peak under Alauddin Khilji, who expanded the territory through successful military campaigns in Gujarat, Rajasthan, Malwa, and the Deccan region. His conquests made the Delhi Sultanate one of the most powerful kingdoms in India at that time.

Religion

The Khilji rulers followed Islam and promoted Islamic institutions. Mosques and madrasas were supported during their rule. However, like other Delhi Sultans, they ruled over a largely Hindu population and maintained administrative systems to govern diverse communities.

Economy and Administration

Alauddin Khilji introduced major economic reforms. He controlled market prices of essential goods to prevent inflation and ensure fair distribution, especially for the army. Strict revenue policies were implemented to strengthen the treasury. Land taxes were collected efficiently, and corruption was controlled through strict supervision of officials.

Notable Rulers

  • Jalal-ud-din Firuz Khilji – Founder of the Khilji Dynasty.
  • Alauddin Khilji – Expanded the empire and introduced important economic reforms.
  • Qutb-ud-din Mubarak Shah – Last effective ruler before the dynasty declined.

Political History and Achievements

The Khilji Dynasty is known for military expansion and defense against Mongol invasions. Alauddin Khilji successfully defended India from repeated Mongol attacks. He also conducted southern expeditions led by his general Malik Kafur, which brought immense wealth to the Delhi Sultanate. Administrative reforms strengthened central authority.

Cultural Heritage

Tangible Heritage

The Khilji period contributed to Indo-Islamic architecture. The famous Alai Darwaza in the Qutub complex is an important example of early Islamic architecture in India. It shows the use of red sandstone and white marble, intricate carvings, and true arches.

Intangible Heritage

The Khilji era influenced administrative systems, military organization, and market regulation policies. Their governance style strengthened centralized rule. Historical accounts and Persian chronicles describe their rule, campaigns, and economic reforms in detail.

Decline and Fall

After the death of Alauddin Khilji, weak successors and internal conflicts weakened the dynasty. Political instability increased, and in 1320 CE, Ghiyas-ud-din Tughlaq overthrew the last Khilji ruler, marking the beginning of the Tughlaq Dynasty.

Historical Sources and Evidence

Information about the Khilji Dynasty comes from Persian chronicles, inscriptions, coins, and historical works such as writings by Amir Khusrau. Archaeological monuments like Alai Darwaza and other structures in Delhi provide material evidence of their architectural contributions.
