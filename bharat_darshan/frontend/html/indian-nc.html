Formation of Indian National Congress (1885 CE)

Year: 1885 CE | Region: Bombay (Mumbai)

Background

By the late 19th century, educated Indians—especially in cities such as Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras—had begun to seek political reform and greater representation in governance. Growing discontent with British rule, economic exploitation, and discriminatory policies, combined with the influence of Western education and liberal ideas, encouraged political unity among Indians from different regions and communities. Earlier associations such as the Indian Association (founded in 1876 by Surendranath Banerjee and Ananda Mohan Bose) and various provincial political groups had already laid the groundwork for an all-India organization.

British officials and Indian intellectuals alike saw a need for a formal platform where educated Indians could voice their grievances and discuss constitutional reforms, leading to the idea of a national political body that would later become the Indian National Congress.

Founding

The Indian National Congress (INC) was founded in 1885 by Allan Octavian Hume, a retired British civil servant who had long advocated for political dialogue between Indians and the colonial government. The first session was held in Bombay (now Mumbai) from 28 to 31 December 1885 at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, attended by 72 delegates from across British India. These delegates represented different provinces, presidencies, and communities, and included lawyers, journalists, teachers, and social reformers.

Hume played a key role as the first General Secretary of the Congress, while Womesh Chunder Bonnerjee was elected as the first President, presiding over the inaugural session. The Congress began as a moderate, loyalist association that sought reform within the framework of the British Empire rather than immediate independence.

Objectives

  • To promote political dialogue, friendship, and mutual understanding among educated Indians from different regions and communities, helping to build a sense of national unity.
  • To create a national platform for discussing Indian interests under British rule, including demands for civil liberties, administrative reforms, and greater Indian participation in government.
  • To train Indians in the art of self-governance through peaceful and constitutional means, such as petitions, resolutions, and public meetings, rather than violent confrontation.

Prominent Early Leaders

  • Dadabhai Naoroji: Known as the “Grand Old Man of India,” he was a leading moderate who articulated the “Drain of Wealth” theory, arguing that British policies were draining India’s resources and impoverishing its people.
  • W.C. Bonnerjee: First President of the Indian National Congress, he helped define the early aims of the organization and presided over its first session in Bombay.
  • Surendranath Banerjee: A pioneering nationalist leader who promoted unity among Indians and organized early political conferences that paved the way for the Congress.
  • Gopal Krishna Gokhale: A moderate reformer who emphasized gradual political change, education, and social reform as the path to self-government.

Impact and Significance

  • The formation of the Indian National Congress marked the beginning of an organized national movement in India, providing a unified political platform for Indians across regions and communities.
  • It brought together leaders from different linguistic, religious, and social backgrounds, helping to create a shared political identity and laying the foundation for future mass movements.
  • The Congress gradually evolved from a moderate, elite-based association into the principal vehicle of India’s struggle for independence, leading major campaigns such as Swadeshi, Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, and Quit India.
  • The organization played a central role in shaping modern Indian democracy, as it dominated the freedom struggle and later formed many of India’s governments after independence in 1947.
