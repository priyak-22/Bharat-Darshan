Formation of Indian National Congress (1885 CE)

Year: 1885 CE | Region: Bombay (Mumbai)

Background

By the late 19th century, educated Indians began seeking political reform and representation in governance. Growing discontent with British rule and the influence of Western education encouraged political unity among Indians, leading to the formation of a national political organization.

Founding

The Indian National Congress (INC) was founded in 1885 by Allan Octavian Hume, a retired British civil servant. The first session was held in Bombay from December 28 to 31, 1885, and was attended by 72 delegates from across India.

Objectives

  • To promote political dialogue and understanding among educated Indians.
  • To create a national platform for discussing Indian interests under British rule.
  • To train Indians in the art of self-governance through peaceful and constitutional means.

Prominent Early Leaders

  • Dadabhai Naoroji – Advocate of economic reform and “Drain of Wealth” theory.
  • W.C. Bonnerjee – First President of the Indian National Congress.
  • Surendranath Banerjee – Early nationalist leader promoting unity and reform.
  • Gopal Krishna Gokhale – Moderate reformer advocating gradual political change.

Impact and Significance

  • Marked the beginning of an organized national movement in India.
  • Brought together leaders from different regions and communities.
  • Laid the foundation for India’s struggle for independence.
