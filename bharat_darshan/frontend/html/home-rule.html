Home Rule Movement (1916–1918 CE)

Period: 1916–1918 CE | Region: All India (focused in Maharashtra and Madras)

Background

After years of limited reforms under British rule, Indian leaders began demanding self-governance or "Home Rule" similar to the dominion status of colonies like Canada and Australia. The movement grew during World War I, when Indian resources were heavily used for the British war effort.

Leaders and Organizations

  • Bal Gangadhar Tilak – Founded the Home Rule League in April 1916 at Pune, focusing on Maharashtra and Central India.
  • Annie Besant – Established another League in September 1916 at Madras, working across southern India.

Objectives

  • To attain self-government within the British Empire.
  • To increase political awareness and participation among Indians.
  • To unite Indians across regions and religions for a common political cause.

Methods and Activities

  • Organized public meetings, lectures, and publications to spread awareness.
  • Used newspapers and pamphlets to promote the idea of Home Rule.
  • Encouraged local self-government and civic engagement.

Impact and Significance

  • Revived political activity after the decline of the Swadeshi Movement.
  • Strengthened national unity and prepared ground for the Non-Cooperation Movement.
  • Made self-rule a widely accepted political goal among Indians.
