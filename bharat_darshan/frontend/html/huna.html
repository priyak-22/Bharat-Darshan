Huna Invasions (Hephthalites)

Period: 5th – 6th century CE | Region: Northwestern India

Capital

Ruled from various northern and northwestern strongholds (Punjab and Gandhara regions)

Notable Rulers

Toramana and Mihirakula

Religion

The Hunas followed a mix of local Hindu and Central Asian practices, though some were hostile to Buddhism under Mihirakula’s rule.

Administration

The Hunas established short-lived kingdoms across northwestern India. Their rule lacked a centralized structure and was marked by military dominance and local governance by chieftains. They disrupted trade and learning centers during their invasions.

Achievements / Impact

  • Accelerated the decline of the Gupta Empire
  • Introduced Central Asian military traditions to Indian warfare
  • Led to the rise of regional kingdoms in post-Gupta India
  • Eventually defeated by Yashodharman of Malwa and Narasimhagupta Baladitya
