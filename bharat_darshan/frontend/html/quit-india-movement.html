Quit India Movement (1942 CE)

Period: 1942 CE | Region: All India

Background

During World War II, the British dragged India into the conflict without consulting Indian leaders. The failure of the Cripps Mission (1942) to grant genuine self-government led to widespread frustration and the launch of the Quit India Movement.

Launch and Leadership

  • Launched by the Indian National Congress under the leadership of Mahatma Gandhi on August 8, 1942 in Mumbai (then Bombay).
  • In his famous speech, Gandhi gave the call of “Do or Die”.
  • Prominent leaders like Jawaharlal Nehru, Sardar Patel, Maulana Azad and others supported the movement.

Main Features

  • Mass civil disobedience across the country demanding immediate British withdrawal from India.
  • Formation of underground networks after the arrest of top leaders.
  • Strikes, boycotts, and sabotage of communication and transport lines.
  • Participation from students, workers, and peasants in rural and urban areas.

Key Events

  • 8 August 1942: Gandhi’s “Quit India” resolution passed at the Bombay session.
  • 9 August 1942: Major Congress leaders arrested immediately after the launch.
  • Violent clashes and suppression followed across India.
  • Emergence of local parallel governments in places like Ballia, Satara, and Tamluk.

Impact and Significance

  • Marked the final mass movement in India’s struggle for freedom.
  • Demonstrated the determination of Indians to achieve complete independence.
  • Though suppressed, it convinced the British that their rule in India could not continue for long.
  • Set the stage for independence achieved in 1947.
