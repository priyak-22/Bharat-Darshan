Chandela & Chauhan Dynasties (c. 7th–13th century CE)

Regions: Bundelkhand (Central India) and Rajasthan–Delhi region (North India)

Chandela Dynasty

The Chandela rulers governed the Bundelkhand region between the 9th and 13th centuries. They are best known for constructing the magnificent :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}, famous for their intricate sculptures and temple architecture.

Capital

The main capitals were Khajuraho and Mahoba. These cities became important political and cultural centers in central India.

Notable Rulers

  • Nannuka – Founder of the dynasty.
  • Dhanga – Expanded the kingdom and promoted temple construction.
  • Vidyadhara – Defended the kingdom against Mahmud of Ghazni.

Cultural Heritage

Tangible Heritage

Khajuraho Temples

Built between the 10th and 12th centuries, these temples represent the peak of Nagara-style architecture.

Intangible Heritage

The Chandela period is known for temple rituals, artistic traditions, and Sanskrit inscriptions that highlight cultural growth.

Chauhan Dynasty

The Chauhans were a major Rajput dynasty that ruled parts of Rajasthan and Delhi from the 7th to 12th centuries. Their most famous ruler was :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}.

Capital

The primary centers of power were Ajmer and Delhi. Ajmer served as the early capital before expansion toward northern India.

Notable Rulers

  • Vasudeva – Early founder of the Chauhan line.
  • Ajayaraja II – Founder of Ajmer city.
  • Prithviraj Chauhan – Known for the Battles of Tarain against Muhammad Ghori.

Political History

The Chauhans resisted early Turkish invasions. After the Second Battle of Tarain in 1192, their power declined and Delhi came under Ghurid control.

Cultural Contributions

The dynasty supported temples, forts, and Sanskrit learning. Heroic ballads like Prithviraj Raso later celebrated their legacy.
