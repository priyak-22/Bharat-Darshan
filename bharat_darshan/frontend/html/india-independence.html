Independence of India (15 August 1947 CE)

Period: 1947 CE | Region: All India

Background

After nearly two centuries of British rule and a long struggle led by countless freedom fighters, India finally achieved independence on 15 August 1947. [web:214][web:221] The movement for independence had gained unstoppable momentum after the Quit India Movement of 1942, and the weakening of Britain after World War II made it increasingly difficult for the colonial government to hold on to its empire. [web:209][web:219] The failure of the Cabinet Mission Plan and the rise of communal tensions led the British government to accept the idea of partition and transfer of power to two separate dominions—India and Pakistan. [web:214][web:217]

The Indian National Congress, Muslim League, and other political groups negotiated with the British and with each other, ultimately agreeing on a plan that would end British suzerainty and allow Indians to govern themselves. [web:214][web:220]

Transfer of Power

  • British Parliament passed the Indian Independence Act on 18 July 1947, which legally ended British rule and set the stage for the creation of two independent dominions. [web:214][web:219]
  • The Act provided for the partition of British India into two independent dominions: India and Pakistan, effective from 15 August 1947. [web:214][web:217]
  • Both dominions were granted full legislative authority, the right to frame their own constitutions, and autonomy over internal and external affairs, while initially remaining members of the British Commonwealth. [web:214][web:217]
  • Lord Mountbatten served as the last Viceroy of British India and the first Governor‑General of independent India, overseeing the transition of power and the complex process of partition. [web:219][web:221]

The Act also abolished the office of Secretary of State for India and terminated British suzerainty over the princely states, allowing them to join either India or Pakistan based on geographical contiguity and the wishes of their people. [web:214][web:217]

Key Events

  • 15 August 1947: India officially became an independent nation, with the Union Jack lowered and the Indian tricolour hoisted in Delhi and across the country. [web:219][web:221]
  • Jawaharlal Nehru delivered the historic “Tryst with Destiny” speech at midnight in the Constituent Assembly, declaring that India had “awakened to life and freedom” and calling for unity and service to the nation. [web:219][web:222]
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad and Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel played key roles in shaping the early government and national unity, with Patel leading the integration of princely states into the Indian Union. [web:219][web:220]
  • Mass celebrations took place across the country despite the sorrow of partition, as people celebrated the end of colonial rule while grappling with the trauma of displacement and violence. [web:217][web:221]

The Constituent Assembly, which had been functioning since 1946, now began the task of drafting a permanent constitution for the new republic, which would come into force on 26 January 1950. [web:214][web:219]

Partition and Its Aftermath

  • India was divided into India and Pakistan (comprising East and West Pakistan), with the partition of the provinces of Punjab and Bengal along religious lines. [web:214][web:217]
  • The partition led to large‑scale communal violence and one of the biggest migrations in human history, as millions of Hindus, Sikhs, and Muslims crossed the newly drawn borders. [web:217][web:221]
  • Estimates suggest that several hundred thousand to over a million people lost their lives, and tens of millions were displaced, creating deep social and psychological scars that still resonate today. [web:217][web:221]
  • Despite the turmoil, India embarked on a journey as a sovereign democratic republic, determined to build a secular, pluralistic, and inclusive state. [web:219][web:220]

The division of assets, including the armed forces, railways, and financial reserves, was carried out under the supervision of the Partition Council, adding another layer of complexity to the transition. [web:214][web:217]

Significance

  • Independence marked the end of British colonial rule in India and the beginning of self‑governance for over 300 million people. [web:219][web:221]
  • It laid the foundation for the world’s largest democracy, with India adopting a parliamentary system and a written constitution that guaranteed fundamental rights and universal adult franchise. [web:214][web:219]
  • The event became a symbol of hope, unity, and self‑determination for nations under colonial rule, inspiring anti‑colonial movements across Asia, Africa, and the Caribbean. [web:217][web:221]
  • Independence also set the stage for India’s post‑colonial journey, including nation‑building, economic development, and the integration of diverse communities into a single political framework. [web:219][web:220]
