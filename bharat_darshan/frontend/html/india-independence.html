Independence of India (15 August 1947 CE)

Period: 1947 CE | Region: All India

Background

After nearly two centuries of British rule and a long struggle led by countless freedom fighters, India finally achieved independence on 15 August 1947. The movement for independence had gained unstoppable momentum after the Quit India Movement of 1942.

Transfer of Power

  • British Parliament passed the Indian Independence Act on 18 July 1947.
  • The Act provided for the partition of British India into two independent dominions: India and Pakistan.
  • Lord Mountbatten served as the last Viceroy of British India and the first Governor-General of independent India.

Key Events

  • 15 August 1947: India officially became an independent nation.
  • Jawaharlal Nehru delivered the historic “Tryst with Destiny” speech at midnight in the Constituent Assembly.
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad and Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel played key roles in shaping the early government and national unity.
  • Mass celebrations took place across the country despite the sorrow of partition.

Partition and Its Aftermath

  • India was divided into India and Pakistan (comprising East and West Pakistan).
  • The partition led to large-scale communal violence and one of the biggest migrations in human history.
  • Millions were displaced and thousands lost their lives in the process.
  • Despite the turmoil, India embarked on a journey as a sovereign democratic republic.

Significance

  • Marked the end of British colonial rule in India.
  • Laid the foundation for the world’s largest democracy.
  • Became a symbol of hope, unity, and self-determination for nations under colonial rule.
