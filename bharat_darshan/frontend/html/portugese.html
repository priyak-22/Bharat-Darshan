Advent of the Portuguese (1498 CE)

Period: 1498 CE | Region: Western Coastal India (Goa, Daman, Diu, Calicut)

Background

In 1498 CE, Portuguese explorer Vasco da Gama reached Calicut on the Malabar Coast, opening a direct sea route between Europe and India. This marked the beginning of European maritime presence in the Indian subcontinent.

Major Trading Posts

  • Goa: Became the main administrative and trade center of Portuguese India.
  • Daman: A key port for regional control and coastal trade.
  • Diu: Important naval base and strategic fort on the western coast.

Notable Figures

Vasco da Gama: Led the first successful voyage from Europe to India via the Cape of Good Hope.

Afonso de Albuquerque: Expanded Portuguese power by capturing Goa in 1510 CE and securing dominance over Indian Ocean trade routes.

Administration

The Portuguese established fortified trading settlements (factories) governed by captains and viceroys. Their rule was primarily commercial, focusing on control of maritime trade and spice routes rather than extensive territorial conquest.

Impact and Significance

  • Introduced European naval dominance in the Indian Ocean.
  • Disrupted existing Arab and Indian merchant trade networks.
  • Led to spread of Christianity and European architectural influence in coastal India.
  • Marked the beginning of a new era of European colonization in India.
