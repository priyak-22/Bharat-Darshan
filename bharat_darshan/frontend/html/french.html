Advent of the French (1664 CE)

Period: 1664 CE | Region: Pondicherry, Chandernagore, Mahe, Karaikal

Background

The French East India Company was founded in 1664 CE under King Louis XIV to expand French influence and trade in Asia. The French aimed to compete with the British and Dutch for control of Indian Ocean commerce and established key trading centers along the Indian coast.

Major Trading Posts

  • Pondicherry: Became the main French headquarters in India and a major center of administration and trade.
  • Chandernagore: Established near Calcutta in Bengal as a key trading post for textiles and indigo.
  • Mahe: Served as a French port on the Malabar Coast for spice trade.
  • Karaikal: Supported trade activities on the Coromandel Coast and connected inland routes.

Administration

The French East India Company appointed governors to manage its Indian territories. Pondicherry served as the capital, and Governor Joseph François Dupleix played a major role in expanding French power and diplomacy in southern India.

Trade and Economy

  • Focused on the export of silk, indigo, saltpetre, and spices.
  • Imported French goods such as wine, metalware, and luxury items to India.
  • Established strong commercial links between India and Southeast Asia.

Decline and Impact

  • Faced fierce rivalry with the British East India Company during the Carnatic Wars.
  • Lost major battles to the British by the mid-18th century, leading to reduced influence.
  • Retained control of small territories like Pondicherry until the mid-20th century.
  • Contributed to the spread of French culture, architecture, and education in India.
