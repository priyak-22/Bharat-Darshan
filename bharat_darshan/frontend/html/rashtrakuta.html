Rashtrakuta Dynasty (c. 753–982 CE)

Region: Deccan Plateau — Karnataka and Maharashtra

Capital

The Rashtrakutas ruled primarily from Manyakheta (modern Malkhed in Karnataka). Their strategic location in the Deccan allowed them to control important trade routes connecting northern and southern India.

Background

The Rashtrakutas rose to power in the 8th century after overthrowing the Chalukyas of Badami. They established one of the most powerful empires in the Deccan and became major participants in North Indian politics.

Founding and Rise

Dantidurga founded the dynasty around 753 CE. Under Krishna I and later Govinda III, the empire expanded significantly. They became key players in the Tripartite Struggle for control of Kannauj.

Religion

The Rashtrakutas were patrons of Hinduism, Jainism, and Buddhism. Many rulers supported Shaivism and Vaishnavism, while Jain scholars received royal patronage. Religious tolerance was a defining feature of their rule.

Economy and Administration

The economy was based on agriculture, trade, and temple-centered activities. They maintained strong maritime trade connections with the Arab world. The administration was organized into provinces governed by trusted officials.

Notable Rulers

  • Dantidurga – Founder of the Rashtrakuta power.
  • Krishna I – Builder of the Kailasa Temple at Ellora.
  • Govinda III – Expanded the empire across northern India.
  • Amoghavarsha I – Promoted literature and Jain scholarship.

Political History and Achievements

The Rashtrakutas were deeply involved in the Tripartite Struggle with the Palas and Gurjara-Pratiharas for dominance over northern India. Their reign marked a golden period of Deccan political and cultural history.

Cultural Heritage

Tangible Heritage

Kailasa Temple, Ellora

The Kailasa Temple is one of the greatest monolithic rock-cut temples in the world. Carved from a single rock, it represents architectural and engineering brilliance.

Ellora Caves

The Rashtrakutas contributed significantly to the development of the Ellora cave complex, which includes Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain monuments.

Rashtrakuta Inscriptions

Numerous inscriptions provide valuable historical evidence about administration, land grants, and religious patronage.

Intangible Heritage

The Rashtrakutas promoted Sanskrit and Kannada literature. Amoghavarsha I authored the famous Kannada work "Kavirajamarga." Their patronage strengthened regional language development and literary culture in the Deccan.

Decline and Fall

Internal conflicts and external invasions weakened the empire. By the late 10th century, the Western Chalukyas replaced Rashtrakuta authority in the Deccan region.

Historical Sources and Evidence

Information about the Rashtrakutas comes from inscriptions, temple architecture, literary works such as Kavirajamarga, and Arab travel accounts describing trade relations.
