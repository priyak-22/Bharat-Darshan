British Expansion & Administration (1764–1857 CE)

Period: 1764–1857 CE | Region: Across India (Bengal, Mysore, Maratha, Punjab)

Background

Following their victories at Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764), the British East India Company transitioned from a trading power to a territorial authority. Over the next century, the Company expanded its control through wars, diplomacy, and administrative reforms.

Methods of Expansion

  • Subsidiary Alliance (1798 CE): Introduced by Lord Wellesley, forcing Indian rulers to accept British troops in their territories and pay for their maintenance, effectively bringing them under British control.
  • Doctrine of Lapse: Policy of annexation introduced by Lord Dalhousie, allowing the British to annex any princely state without a male heir (e.g., Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur).
  • Wars of Conquest: Series of battles against Indian powers such as Mysore, the Marathas, and the Sikhs that consolidated British authority.

Major Conquests

  • Mysore Wars (1767–1799): Defeat and death of Tipu Sultan led to British control of southern India.
  • Maratha Wars (1775–1818): Ended Maratha power and established British supremacy over western India.
  • Anglo-Sikh Wars (1845–1849): Resulted in the annexation of Punjab.

Administration

The British introduced centralized administration, modern legal and revenue systems, and restructured the army. They also established the Civil Services to manage governance, while policies favored British trade and industries.

Impact and Significance

  • Unified large parts of India under a single political authority.
  • Led to economic exploitation and drain of wealth to Britain.
  • Transformed traditional Indian political systems and economies.
  • Created growing discontent that culminated in the Revolt of 1857.
