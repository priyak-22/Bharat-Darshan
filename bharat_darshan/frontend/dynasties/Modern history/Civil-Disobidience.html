Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–1934 CE)

Period: 1930–1934 CE | Region: All India

Background

After the failure of earlier constitutional reforms and the Simon Commission (1927) boycott, Mahatma Gandhi launched the Civil Disobedience Movement to protest against British laws and assert the right to self-rule.

Launch and Leadership

  • Launched by Mahatma Gandhi on March 12, 1930 with the historic Dandi March.
  • Gandhi, along with 78 followers, marched from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi (about 390 km) to produce salt in defiance of the British salt monopoly.
  • The movement spread rapidly across India involving millions of people.

Main Features

  • Refusal to pay taxes and obey unjust laws.
  • Boycott of British goods, courts, and institutions.
  • Resignation from government services and municipal positions.
  • Peaceful protests, picketing, and demonstrations.

Key Events

  • Dandi March (1930): Symbol of defiance against British salt laws.
  • Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931): Gandhi agreed to suspend the movement and attend the Second Round Table Conference in London.
  • Movement resumed in 1932 after the failure of talks.

Impact and Significance

  • Brought the demand for complete independence to the forefront.
  • Mobilized women, students, and peasants on a large scale.
  • Strengthened Gandhi’s position as the national leader.
  • Weakened British authority and inspired global anti-colonial struggles.
