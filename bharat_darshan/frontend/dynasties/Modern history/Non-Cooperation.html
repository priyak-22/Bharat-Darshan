Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922 CE)

Period: 1920–1922 CE | Region: All India

Background

The Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919) and the repressive Rowlatt Act deeply angered Indians. The failure of the British to meet post–World War I promises of self-government led Mahatma Gandhi to call for peaceful non-cooperation with British institutions.

Launch and Leadership

  • Launched by Mahatma Gandhi in 1920.
  • Supported by leaders like Motilal Nehru, Chittaranjan Das, Lala Lajpat Rai, and Maulana Azad.
  • Endorsed at the Nagpur Session of the Indian National Congress (1920).

Major Programmes

  • Boycott of British schools, courts, and offices.
  • Boycott of foreign goods and promotion of Swadeshi (use of Indian-made goods).
  • Resignation from government posts and return of British titles and honors.
  • Encouragement of Khadi and village industries.

Public Response

The movement spread rapidly across India, involving students, peasants, and workers. It gave the freedom struggle a mass character for the first time.

Suspension of the Movement

In February 1922, the Chauri Chaura incident in Uttar Pradesh, where protesters killed policemen, led Gandhi to suspend the movement, emphasizing his commitment to non-violence.

Impact and Significance

  • Brought Gandhi to the forefront of Indian politics.
  • Unified Indians across class and community lines.
  • Marked the beginning of the Gandhian era in India’s freedom struggle.
  • Prepared the ground for future mass movements like Civil Disobedience.
